How can we solve the portfolio switching anxiety users feel when migrating from their original investment platform — and how do we design a home savings feature that meets the real needs of younger adults?

01

Challenge

Understanding a new kind of saver

Kron's existing users were engaged investors — but a large and growing segment had a fundamentally different goal: saving for a home. This group brought different motivations, different anxieties, and a different relationship with money.

The challenge was to design a feature that felt native to Kron while serving a mindset the platform hadn't fully addressed before — goal-driven, emotionally charged, and long-horizon.

"Where do we even start?" — The brief was deliberately open. My job was to define the problem space before designing any solution.
Research
02

Research

Uncovering pains and barriers

I ran a research sprint across user interviews, competitor analysis, and platform analytics to understand the emotional and practical barriers younger adults face when thinking about saving for a home through an investment platform.

01

User interviews

Spoke with Kron users and non-users aged 22–32 about their home-saving habits, anxieties, and current tools. Uncovered a consistent fear of "locking money away" and confusion around investment risk vs. savings goals.

02

Competitor mapping

Analysed how competing platforms (DNB, Sbanken, Bulder) positioned home savings. Found a gap: most treated it as a pure savings product, not an emotionally resonant goal.

03

Platform analytics

Worked with the product team to identify drop-off points and portfolio-switching behaviour among new users migrating from other platforms.

04

Synthesis

Mapped findings into insight themes that directly shaped the design direction and feature scope.

Key Insights

Four things research kept confirming

01

Younger adults don't separate "saving" from "investing" — they need one coherent experience.

02

Home saving is emotionally loaded. Users want to feel progress, not just see numbers.

03

Portfolio switching creates anxiety. Users fear making the "wrong" choice when migrating.

04

Trust is built through transparency. Users want to understand why a recommendation is made.

"

"I don't really know if I should keep my money in funds or just save normally. It feels like a big decision and I'm scared of getting it wrong."

— User interview, age 26

Solution
03

Solution

A home savings feature built on clarity

The solution centred on a dedicated home savings flow within Kron — designed to reduce decision anxiety, surface relevant portfolio recommendations, and make progress feel real and motivating.

Solution designs

Home savings feature — key screens

✓
A goal-setting onboarding flow that frames investing in terms of life milestones, not financial jargon
✓
Transparent portfolio recommendations with plain-language explanations of risk and return
✓
A progress view that shows users how their savings track against their home goal
✓
A simplified migration flow that reduces portfolio switching anxiety with guided decision-making

Testing

Test, iterate, test more

Prototypes were tested in two rounds with users matching the target profile. Key findings shaped significant changes between rounds — particularly around how portfolio risk was communicated and how the goal-setting flow was ordered.

Testing rounds

Prototype testing — two rounds, six participants each

The final designs were reviewed by the Kron product team and presented at executive level. The work was recognised by Storebrand leadership for quality, clarity, and strategic relevance.
Reflection

Learnings

What I took away

Start with the emotion, not the feature

The most important design decision was reframing the feature around a life goal rather than a product category. That shift changed everything downstream.

Data and intuition work together

Analytics told us where users dropped off. Interviews told us why. Neither alone was enough — the combination shaped the most impactful decisions.

Clarity is a design output

The feedback from leadership centred on clarity. That confirmed that simplifying complexity is as valuable as any visual craft skill.

Velocity doesn't mean shortcuts

Delivering executive-ready work in a summer internship timeframe taught me how to prioritise ruthlessly without sacrificing quality.
